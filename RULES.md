# Texas Hold'em — Official Rules

**English** | [Русский](RULES.ru.md)

These are the rules of **No-Limit Texas Hold'em** as it is played in tournaments. They follow the
**Poker TDA Rules, 2024 edition (Version 1.0, October 9, 2024)**, published by the Poker Tournament
Directors Association ([pokertda.com](https://www.pokertda.com)). Most major tournament series use
this standard.

How to read the references:

- **(TDA 43)** — the number of the TDA rule this point is based on. **(TDA RP-5)** — a TDA
  Recommended Procedure.
- **(common practice)** — a widely used convention that the TDA rulebook does not write down, such
  as hand rankings or the order of the deal.
- **🎰 Live only** — a situation that can only happen with physical cards and chips (exposed cards,
  misdeals, string bets). A software dealer never makes these mistakes, but the rules are included
  for completeness.

## 1. Overview

- **Players:** 2 to 10 per table. A full tournament table usually seats 9 players. Common formats
  are 9-max, 8-max, 6-max ("short-handed") and heads-up (2 players).
- **Goal:** win chips. You win a pot either by making the best five-card hand at showdown, or by
  betting so that every other player folds.
- **Hole cards:** each player gets **2 private cards**.
- **Community cards (the board):** up to **5 shared cards**, dealt face up in the middle of the table.
- **Making a hand:** you use the **best 5 of the 7 cards** available to you (2 hole cards + 5 board
  cards). You may use both hole cards, one, or none.
- **Betting structure:** **No-Limit**. A player may bet any amount from the minimum up to all of
  their chips at any time.

## 2. Cards and Deck

- **Exactly one standard 52-card deck** is used, with no jokers. Every card exists only once.
- The deck is **shuffled before every hand**. Each hand is dealt from a complete 52-card deck.
- **Duplicate cards are not allowed.** If two or more cards of the same rank and suit turn up, the
  deck is **fouled**: play stops and all bets are returned (TDA 35-E). Non-standard cards, such as
  jokers, cause a misdeal (TDA 35-A).
- **Multi-deck shoes (for example, 6 decks) are not used in poker.** Those belong to blackjack and
  baccarat. In Hold'em, a second deck would allow impossible hands such as five aces, or a flush made
  of two identical cards.
  - Some cardrooms keep **two decks with different backs** and alternate them: one is in play while
    the other is being shuffled. Each hand is still dealt from **one** deck.
- **4 suits:** ♠ spades, ♥ hearts, ♦ diamonds, ♣ clubs.
- **13 ranks** from high to low:

  `A  K  Q  J  T(10)  9  8  7  6  5  4  3  2`

- The **Ace** is the highest card, but it can also be low in exactly one hand: the straight
  `A-2-3-4-5` (the "wheel"), which is a five-high straight.
- **Suits have no rank** when comparing hands. A flush in hearts is exactly equal to a flush in
  spades with the same ranks.
  - Suits are used only for **draws**, for example to assign seats when a table breaks: the highest
    card wins, and ties are broken ♠ > ♥ > ♦ > ♣ (TDA Addendum, Rule 10).

## 3. Table Positions

The **dealer button** marks the nominal dealer. Play always moves **clockwise**.

| Position | Description |
|---|---|
| **Button (BTN)** | The nominal dealer. Acts last on every betting round after the flop. |
| **Small Blind (SB)** | First seat to the left of the button. Posts the small blind. |
| **Big Blind (BB)** | Second seat to the left of the button. Posts the big blind. |
| **Under the Gun (UTG)** | First seat to the left of the big blind. Acts first before the flop. |
| Middle positions (MP) | Seats between UTG and the hijack. |
| **Hijack (HJ)** | Second seat to the right of the button. |
| **Cutoff (CO)** | First seat to the right of the button. |

- **Seats** in a tournament are assigned **at random** (TDA 7).
- **Starting button:** decided by a high-card draw, with ties broken by suit (♠ > ♥ > ♦ > ♣)
  *(common practice)*.
- After each hand the button moves **one seat clockwise** (see the dead button rule in §4).

### Heads-up (2 players)

- The **button is the small blind**, is dealt the **last card**, acts **first before the flop** and
  **last on every later round** (TDA 34-B).
- When play becomes heads-up, the button may need to be adjusted so that no player posts the big
  blind twice in a row (TDA 34-B).

## 4. Blinds and Antes

**Blinds** are forced bets posted before any cards are dealt. They make sure every pot has something
to win.

- **Small blind** is usually half the big blind.
- **Big blind** sets the minimum bet for the whole hand.
- Blinds count toward the player's bet in the first betting round. A player who posted a blind only
  needs to add the difference to call.

### Antes

An **ante** is an extra forced bet that adds to the pot without counting toward any player's
betting.

- **Big Blind Ante (BBA)** is the recommended tournament format (TDA RP-11). The player in the big
  blind pays the ante **for the whole table**, usually equal to the big blind.
  - **The big blind is paid first** ("big-blind-first"). If the player cannot cover both, whatever
    chips remain after the blind go to the ante (TDA RP-11).
- **Traditional ante:** every player at the table posts an equal ante before the deal. Some events
  still use it.
- The ante is **dead money**. It goes straight into the pot and does not count toward calling or
  raising.
- Antes are **not reduced** as the tournament goes on, including at the final table (TDA RP-11).

### Dead Button (tournaments)

- Tournaments use a **dead button** (TDA 32). The **big blind moves forward exactly one seat** each
  hand, so nobody skips the big blind and nobody posts it twice in a row.
- After eliminations, the button may end up on an empty seat (a dead button), or there may be no
  small blind for one hand.
- Players who deliberately dodge a blind are penalized (TDA 33).

### Short Blinds

If a player has fewer chips than the required blind, they post everything they have and are
**all-in** (see §9).

## 5. Dealing a Hand

1. The deck is **shuffled** and **cut**. 🎰
2. **Hole cards:** starting with the first seat to the left of the button (the small blind) and going
   clockwise, each player gets one card face down. The deal goes around again so everyone ends up
   with **2 cards**. **The button gets the last card.** *(Common practice; heads-up: TDA 34-B.)*
3. **Pre-flop** betting round.
4. **Burn** one card (face down), then deal the **Flop**: 3 board cards face up.
5. **Flop** betting round.
6. Burn one card, then deal the **Turn**: the 4th board card.
7. **Turn** betting round.
8. Burn one card, then deal the **River**: the 5th board card.
9. **River** betting round.
10. **Showdown**, if two or more players are still in the hand.

- **Exactly one card is burned per street, never more** (TDA 38).
- If everyone except one player folds at any point, that player **wins the pot immediately**. They do
  not have to show their cards (TDA 17-B).
- **No "rabbit hunting":** cards that would have come out if the hand had continued are not shown
  (TDA 28).

**Cards used per hand:** `2 × players + 3 burn cards + 5 board cards`. With 10 players that is
28 cards, so one deck is always enough.

## 6. Betting Rounds

| Round | Board | Who acts first |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-flop** | — | UTG (first seat left of the BB). Heads-up: the button/SB |
| **Flop** | 3 cards | First active player left of the button |
| **Turn** | 4 cards | Same as the flop |
| **River** | 5 cards | Same as the flop |

- Action moves **clockwise**. Each player acts in turn (TDA 50).
- **Pre-flop**, the posted big blind counts as the **opening bet** (TDA 51-B). If nobody raises, the
  big blind still gets the **option** to check or raise when the action reaches them.
- A betting round ends when:
  - every player still in the hand has put in the **same amount** (or is all-in), **and**
  - every player has had a chance to act, **and** the last bet or raise has been called (or every
    player checked).
- Players who are all-in take no further part in the betting.

## 7. Player Actions

| Action | When available | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Check** | No bet to face in this round | Pass the action without putting in chips |
| **Bet** | Nobody has bet yet in this round | Put in the first wager of the round |
| **Call** | Facing a bet | Match the current highest bet |
| **Raise** | Facing a bet | Increase the current bet |
| **Fold** | Any time it is your turn | Give up the hand and any claim to the pot |
| **All-in** | Any time it is your turn | Put in all your remaining chips |

The official terms are **bet, raise, call, fold, check, all-in**. Tapping the table counts as a
check (TDA 3).

### Binding actions

- **Action in turn is binding.** Chips that go into the pot stay in the pot (TDA 50-A).
- **General verbal declarations** in turn, such as "call" or "raise", commit the player to the full
  current action (TDA 51-A).
- **Wrong declarations** (TDA 55):
  - saying "call" when there is no bet counts as a **check**;
  - saying "raise" when there is no bet commits the player to at least a **minimum bet**;
  - saying "check" when facing a bet lets the player **call or fold, but not raise**.
- **Folding when you could check**, or folding out of turn, is a **binding fold** and may be
  penalized (TDA 58).
- **Undercall** (putting in less than the call amount without saying "call") must be made up to a
  **full call** heads-up, or when facing the opening bet of a round. In other situations the TD
  decides (TDA 51-B).

### Action out of turn 🎰 (TDA 53)

- The action goes back to the player whose turn it really is.
- If the **action does not change** for the out-of-turn player (the players before them only check,
  call or fold), their out-of-turn action is **binding**.
- If the **action changes** (someone before them bets or raises), the out-of-turn action is **not
  binding**. Their chips are returned and they may call, raise or fold.
- A **fold out of turn is always binding**.
- A skipped player must speak up. If they do not, and substantial action happens after them, the
  out-of-turn action stands.

## 8. No-Limit Betting Rules

### Minimum bet

- The minimum bet in **any** round equals the **big blind**.
- If a player has less than that, they may bet all their chips (all-in).

### Minimum raise (TDA 43)

- A raise must be **at least equal to the largest prior full bet or raise of the current betting
  round**. What counts is the **increment** (how much the last full raise added), not the total bet.

> **Example (TDA Addendum, Rule 43):** blinds 100/200, after the flop.
> - A bets **600**.
> - B raises to **1,600** (a raise of 1,000).
> - C re-raises to **3,600** (a raise of 2,000).
> - The minimum re-raise for D is **5,600**: 3,600 + 2,000. It is **not** 3,600 + 3,600.

- **Maximum bet:** all of the player's chips on the table.
- **Number of raises:** **unlimited** in No-Limit (TDA 48).
- **An underraise** (a raise smaller than the legal minimum) is corrected to the minimum any time
  during the current betting round (TDA 52-A).

### Short all-in and reopening the betting (TDA 47)

- **An all-in (or several short all-ins added together) that is less than a full bet or raise does
  not reopen the betting** for players who have already acted and are not facing at least a full
  bet or raise when the action returns to them. Those players may only **call or fold**.
- Players who **have not yet acted** in this round may call, fold or raise as usual.
- **Short all-ins add up.** If together they reach a full raise, the betting is reopened.
- **The minimum raise is always the last full valid bet or raise of the round.** Short all-ins do
  not increase it.

> **Example 1 (TDA Addendum, Rule 43):** blinds 50/100, pre-flop. UTG goes all-in for 150, which
> adds 50 and is not a full raise. The largest full bet is still the big blind of 100, so the next
> player's minimum raise is **250**.
>
> **Example 2 (TDA Addendum, Rule 47):** blinds 50/100, after the flop.
> - A bets 100.
> - B goes all-in for 125, and C calls 125.
> - D goes all-in for 200, and E calls 200.
>
> A now faces **100 more**. That is a full raise (25 + 75 together), so **A may fold, call or
> raise**. If A only calls, C faces just 75 more. That is not a full raise, so **C may only call or
> fold**.
>
> **Example 3 (TDA Addendum, Rule 47):** blinds 2,000/4,000, pre-flop. A calls 4,000. C goes all-in
> for 7,500 (3,500 more, not a full raise). The big blind has not acted yet, so they may raise to at
> least **11,500**. If the big blind only calls, A faces 3,500 more, which is not a full raise, so
> **A may only call or fold**.

### How bets and raises are made 🎰

- **Verbal or chips** (TDA 40): a bet is made by declaring it out loud and/or by pushing chips
  forward. Whichever comes **first** defines the bet. If both happen at once, a clear verbal
  declaration takes priority.
- **Stating an amount only** ("two hundred") is the same as silently pushing out that amount
  (TDA 40-C).
- **"Raise" with an amount means the total bet.** If A bets 2,000 and B says "raise, eight
  thousand", B's total bet is **8,000** (TDA 43-B).
- **No string bets.** A raise is made in **one motion** with the full amount, or by **declaring the
  full amount** before putting chips in (TDA 42, 56).
- **50% rule** (TDA 43-A): if a player puts in a raise of **50% or more of the largest prior bet or
  raise**, but less than a minimum raise, they **must make a full minimum raise**. Less than 50%
  counts as a **call**, unless the player said "raise" first or the chips are all they have.

  > Example: the opening bet is 1,000. Silently putting out 1,400 is a **call**, because 400 is less
  > than 50% of 1,000. Putting out 1,500 obliges the player to raise to **2,000**.

- **One oversized chip** (TDA 44): if a player facing a bet puts in a single chip larger than the
  bet and says nothing, it is a **call**. To raise with it, the player must say "raise" before the
  chip touches the table. "Raise" with no amount then means the largest raise that chip covers.
  If there is no bet to face, a silent oversized chip is a bet of the chip's full value.
- **Several chips** (TDA 45): if facing a bet with no declaration, the chips are a **call** if every
  chip is needed to make the call (taking away even one of the smallest chips would leave less than
  the call amount). Otherwise the **50% rule** applies. If those were the player's **last chips**,
  it is an **all-in**, whether or not it reaches 50%.
- **Do not use a bet to get change.** Every chip pushed forward can count as part of the bet
  (TDA 61).

## 9. All-In and Side Pots

- A player can never lose more than the chips they had at the start of the hand. They also **cannot
  win more from each opponent than they put in**.
- If a player goes **all-in** and someone bets more, a **side pot** is created:
  - **Main pot:** each player puts in an amount up to the all-in player's total. The all-in player
    can win only this pot.
  - **Side pot(s):** whatever the other players bet on top of that. Only the players who put chips
    into a side pot can win it.
- With several all-ins at different amounts, there will be **several side pots**.
- **Uncalled bet:** if a bet is larger than anyone could call, the uncalled part **goes back** to
  the bettor *(common practice)*.
- **Each pot is awarded separately** (TDA 21). At showdown, side pots are awarded **first**, then
  the main pot (TDA Addendum, Rule 16).

### How side pots are built

1. Sort the amounts each player put in during the hand, from smallest to largest.
2. For the smallest amount `X`, collect `X` from every player (or everything they put in, if less).
   This makes the first pot. Every player who put in at least `X` and has not folded is eligible
   for it.
3. Take `X` off each remaining amount and repeat with the next amount.
4. Folded players' chips stay in the pots, but those players cannot win them.

> **Example:** A goes all-in for 100, B goes all-in for 300, and C calls 300.
> - Main pot: 100 × 3 = **300**. A, B and C are eligible.
> - Side pot: 200 × 2 = **400**. Only B and C are eligible.

## 10. Showdown

The showdown happens when two or more players are left after the last betting round, or when
everyone left is all-in.

### All-in: everyone shows at once (TDA 16)

- Once a player is **all-in** and **all betting by the other players is finished**, every hand is
  turned **face up immediately**. The remaining board cards are dealt after that.
- Nobody who is all-in, or who called all the betting, may muck their hand without showing it. Hands
  in the main pot and in every side pot must all be shown.

> Example (TDA Addendum, Rule 16): pre-flop, A (the shortest stack) is all-in, and B and C call.
> The cards are **not** turned up yet, because B and C can still bet. On the turn B goes all-in and
> C calls. Now no more betting is possible, so **all three hands are turned up**, and then the river
> is dealt.

### Order of showing when nobody is all-in (TDA 17)

- If there was a **bet on the last round**, the **last aggressor** (the last player to bet or raise
  on that round) shows first.
- If there was **no bet on the last round**, the player who would act first shows first: the
  **first seat to the left of the button**.
- Players may also show or muck their hand spontaneously without waiting their turn.
- If every player except one mucks without showing, the last player wins the pot and **does not
  have to show** (TDA 17-B).

### Right to see a hand (TDA 18)

- If there was a **bet on the river**, any player who **called** it has an **absolute right** to
  see the last aggressor's hand ("the hand they paid to see"). This applies only if the caller has
  shown or kept their own cards.
- A player who mucked without showing loses the right to ask to see any hand.
- Any other request is up to the TD.

### Cards speak (TDA 12–14, 19)

- **Cards speak:** the best hand wins, no matter what a player says about it. A deliberately wrong
  call of your own hand can be penalized (TDA 12).
- **To win a pot, you must table both hole cards** face up so the hand can be read clearly. This
  also applies when you are **playing the board** (TDA 13-A, 19).
- A hand that has gone into the muck and **cannot be identified with 100% certainty** is dead and
  cannot win (TDA 13-B, 14).
- The dealer cannot kill a properly tabled hand that was obviously the winner (TDA 13-C).

## 11. Hand Rankings

*(Common practice. The TDA rules do not restate the hand rankings.)*

Hands from strongest to weakest:

| # | Hand | Description | Example | 5-card combos | Probability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Royal Flush** | A-K-Q-J-T of one suit (the best straight flush) | `A♠ K♠ Q♠ J♠ T♠` | 4 | 0.000154% |
| 2 | **Straight Flush** | Five cards in a row, all the same suit | `9♥ 8♥ 7♥ 6♥ 5♥` | 36 | 0.00139% |
| 3 | **Four of a Kind** (Quads) | Four cards of the same rank | `Q♣ Q♦ Q♥ Q♠ 7♦` | 624 | 0.0240% |
| 4 | **Full House** | Three of a kind plus a pair | `K♠ K♦ K♣ 4♥ 4♠` | 3,744 | 0.144% |
| 5 | **Flush** | Five cards of the same suit, not in a row | `A♦ J♦ 8♦ 6♦ 2♦` | 5,108 | 0.197% |
| 6 | **Straight** | Five cards in a row, mixed suits | `T♣ 9♦ 8♠ 7♥ 6♣` | 10,200 | 0.392% |
| 7 | **Three of a Kind** (Trips / Set) | Three cards of the same rank | `7♠ 7♥ 7♦ K♣ 2♠` | 54,912 | 2.11% |
| 8 | **Two Pair** | Two different pairs | `J♥ J♣ 5♠ 5♦ A♥` | 123,552 | 4.75% |
| 9 | **One Pair** | Two cards of the same rank | `9♠ 9♣ A♦ Q♥ 4♣` | 1,098,240 | 42.3% |
| 10 | **High Card** | None of the above | `A♣ Q♦ 9♠ 6♥ 3♣` | 1,302,540 | 50.1% |

*The numbers are for a random 5-card hand from one 52-card deck, out of 2,598,960 possible.*

### Straight details

- The highest straight is `A-K-Q-J-T` (Broadway). The lowest is `5-4-3-2-A` (the wheel).
- **A straight cannot wrap around the Ace:** `Q-K-A-2-3` is **not** a straight.
- With the Ace low (`5-4-3-2-A`), the straight is **five-high** and loses to `6-5-4-3-2`.

## 12. Comparing Hands and Splitting Pots

### General principle

1. First compare the **hand category** (see the table above).
2. If the categories match, compare the **ranks of the cards that make the hand**.
3. If those match too, compare the **kickers** (the other cards in the five-card hand) from highest
   to lowest.
4. **Only 5 cards count.** A 6th or 7th card never breaks a tie.
5. **Suits never break ties.**
6. If all five cards are equal in rank, the hands **tie** and the pot is **split**.

### Tie-breakers by category

| Hand | Compared by (in order) |
|---|---|
| Straight Flush | Highest card of the straight (`5-4-3-2-A` is the lowest) |
| Four of a Kind | Rank of the quads → kicker |
| Full House | Rank of the trips → rank of the pair |
| Flush | Highest card → 2nd → 3rd → 4th → 5th |
| Straight | Highest card of the straight |
| Three of a Kind | Rank of the trips → 1st kicker → 2nd kicker |
| Two Pair | Higher pair → lower pair → kicker |
| One Pair | Rank of the pair → 1st → 2nd → 3rd kicker |
| High Card | All five cards from highest to lowest |

### Examples

> **Kicker decides:** board `K♠ 9♦ 7♣ 4♥ 2♠`. Player 1 has `K♦ Q♣` and Player 2 has `K♥ J♦`.
> Both have a pair of kings, but the queen kicker beats the jack. **Player 1 wins.**

> **Playing the board:** board `A♠ A♦ K♣ K♥ Q♠`. Player 1 has `J♣ T♣` and Player 2 has `5♦ 3♥`.
> Both play the board: `A-A-K-K-Q`. **Split pot.** Each player must still table both hole cards to
> get a share (TDA 19).

> **Counterfeited:** board `8♠ 8♦ 5♣ 5♥ K♠`. Player 1 has `3♣ 3♦` and Player 2 has `A♥ 2♣`.
> Player 1's best hand is `8-8-5-5-K`, because the threes are no longer part of the best five
> cards. Player 2's best hand is `8-8-5-5-A`. **Player 2 wins with the ace kicker.**

> **Better hand on the same board:** board `9♥ 8♥ 7♣ 2♥ K♠`. Player 1 has `T♦ 6♠`, a ten-high
> straight. Player 2 has `A♥ 3♥`, an ace-high flush. **The flush beats the straight.**

### Splitting the pot and odd chips (TDA 20, 21)

- A tied pot is split **equally** among the winners.
- First, odd chips are **broken down into the smallest denomination in play**. Any chip that still
  cannot be split goes to the winner **in the first seat to the left of the button**.
- Each pot (main and side) is split **separately**.

## 13. Tournament Rules

- **Starting stack:** every player starts with the same number of chips. Late registrants and
  re-entries get a **full starting stack** and a random seat. They are dealt in at once, except when
  the seat is **between the small blind and the button** (TDA 8, 10).
- **Blind levels:** blinds and antes go up on a set schedule. A new level applies from the **next
  hand** (TDA 23).
- **Elimination:** a player who loses all their chips is out of the tournament (unless the event
  allows re-entry).
- **Simultaneous eliminations:** if several players are knocked out in the same hand, the player who
  **started the hand with more chips** finishes higher *(common practice)*.
- **Hand-for-hand** (near the prize places, the "bubble"): all tables play one hand at a time. If
  several players bust on the same hand, they **share** the prize money for the places they
  finished in (TDA RP-8).
- **Table balancing:** the player who would be **big blind next** moves to the **worst position**
  at the new table. That position is never the small blind (TDA 11-A).
- **Chip race (color-up)** (TDA 24): when a small chip denomination is removed, the leftover chips
  are raced off starting from seat 1. **A player cannot be raced out of the tournament.** A player
  who loses their last chips in the race gets one chip of the smallest denomination still in play.
- **Chip visibility** (TDA 25): chips must be stacked so they can be counted. Larger denominations
  must always be visible.
- **At your seat** (TDA 30): a player must be **within reach of their chair** when the last card of
  the initial deal is dealt. If not, the hand is **dead**, and their blinds and antes stay in the pot.
- **Stay at the table:** a player with a live hand, including one who is all-in, must stay at the
  table until the hand ends (TDA 31).
- **Clock** (TDA 29): a player who takes too long can have the **clock called** on them: **25
  seconds plus a 5-second countdown**. When time runs out, the hand is **dead** if the player faces
  a bet, or **checked** if they do not.
- **Chip counts** (TDA 60): a player may ask for a reasonable estimate of an opponent's stack. An
  **exact count** may be requested only by a player **facing an all-in bet, on their turn**. The
  dealer or floor does the counting.
- **Accepted action** (TDA 49): the caller is responsible for knowing the size of the bet they call.
  If someone gives them a wrong amount, the call is still for the correct amount.
- **Floor decisions are final.** Fairness and the best interest of the game come before the literal
  wording of a rule (TDA 1).

## 14. Irregularities 🎰 Live only

These come up only with a live dealer. A software game handles them automatically.

### Misdeal (TDA 35)

A misdeal is declared, and the hand is re-dealt exactly as before (same button, same blinds, no new
players seated), if:

1. two or more **boxed cards** (face-up cards in the deck) appear on the initial deal;
2. the first card is dealt to the wrong seat;
3. cards are dealt to a seat that should not get a hand;
4. a seat that should get a hand is skipped;
5. a player is dealt the wrong number of cards (the button may be dealt two cards in a row,
   TDA 35-B and 37);
6. before substantial action, a card that does not belong in the deck (for example, a joker) is
   found;
7. **either of the first two cards** off the deck, or **any two other hole cards**, are exposed by
   the dealer.

**Substantial action (SA)** (TDA 36) is either **2 actions in turn where at least one puts in chips**
(any 2 actions except 2 checks or 2 folds), or **any 3 actions in turn**. Posted blinds do not count.
**After SA, a misdeal cannot be declared**, and the hand continues (TDA 35-D).

**A fouled deck** (two identical cards) stops play and all bets are returned, even after SA
(TDA 35-E).

### Other

- **Button in the wrong place** (TDA 34-A): corrected if noticed before SA. After SA, play continues.
- **Cards exposed by the player:** may be penalized, but the hand stays live (TDA 68).
- **4-card flop** (TDA 39-A): the 4 cards are scrambled face down. The floor picks one at random as
  the burn card, and the other three become the flop.
- **Premature cards** (TDA RP-5): if the flop, turn or river is dealt before the betting round ends,
  the **burn card stays as the burn**. The premature cards go back into the stub, the stub is
  reshuffled, and the street is dealt again **without another burn**.
- **Disputes:** the reading of a tabled hand can be disputed until the next hand begins. Mistakes in
  counting or awarding the pot can be disputed until substantial action on the next hand (TDA 22).

## 15. Etiquette and Fair Play

- **One player to a hand** (TDA 67): players, in the hand or not, must not discuss live or folded
  hands, give advice or criticize play, or read out a hand that has not been tabled.
- **No collusion** (TDA 69): poker is an individual game. **Soft play** (not playing hard against a
  friend) is penalized. **Chip dumping** and other collusion lead to disqualification.
- **Electronic devices** (TDA 5): a player with a live hand must not use a phone or any other device.
  Strategy apps and charts are not allowed at the table.
- **Protect your hand** (TDA 2, 65): players are responsible for their own cards. If the dealer
  kills a hand by mistake, the player has no redress. 🎰
- **Proper folding:** push your cards forward low to the table. Do not throw them high or expose
  them (TDA 68).
- **Penalties** (TDA 71): verbal warning, a missed hand or missed round, or disqualification.

## 16. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Board** | The community cards in the middle of the table |
| **Burn card** | A card thrown away face down before the flop, turn and river |
| **Button (BTN)** | The marker for the nominal dealer |
| **Blinds** | Forced bets from the two players to the left of the button |
| **Ante** | A forced contribution to the pot that does not count toward a player's bet |
| **BBA** | Big Blind Ante: the player in the big blind pays the ante for the whole table |
| **Hole cards** | A player's 2 private cards |
| **Flop / Turn / River** | The first 3 community cards / the 4th / the 5th |
| **Kicker** | A side card that breaks ties between hands of the same rank |
| **Muck** | The pile of discarded cards, or to throw a hand away |
| **Nuts** | The best possible hand on the current board |
| **Pot** | All the chips bet in the current hand |
| **Side pot** | A separate pot that an all-in player cannot win |
| **Showdown** | Revealing cards at the end of the hand to find the winner |
| **Substantial action (SA)** | 2 actions in turn with chips involved, or any 3 actions in turn (TDA 36) |
| **Heads-up** | Play between two players |
| **Limp** | Calling the big blind before the flop instead of raising |
| **3-bet / 4-bet** | The first re-raise / the next re-raise after it |
| **Set** | Three of a kind made with a pocket pair |
| **Trips** | Three of a kind made with one hole card and a pair on the board |
| **Wheel** | The lowest straight, `5-4-3-2-A` |
| **Broadway** | The highest straight, `A-K-Q-J-T` |
| **Bubble** | The last place before the prize-paying places |
| **TD** | Tournament Director, whose decision is final |
